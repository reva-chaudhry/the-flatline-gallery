ABOUT
THE ARCHIVE

An archive of Ethereum NFT collections that traded during the 2021–2023 boom, but are no longer active as of April 2026. The NFT era produced thousands of projects, most of which have gone quiet. This mausoleum exists to record and learn from that history.

01
Methodology

HOW COLLECTIONS WERE SELECTED

Our methodology focused on closed-lifecycle collections — assets with significant market traction during the 2021–2023 cycle that ceased trading activity prior to 2024, excluding utility-based protocols like ENS and shared marketplace storefronts.

Trading Window
Collections must have traded between February 2021 and September 2023.
Activity & Liquidity
To focus on high-impact projects, we required a minimum of 50 unique transactions and a total economic volume exceeding $1,000 USD.
The Death Criteria
To hone in on completed lifecycles, the final recorded trade for the collection must have occurred before January 1, 2024.
Token Supply
The dataset was limited to collections with a supply of 10,000+ tokens — the dominant format of the time and a consistent, culturally meaningful unit to work with.
Excluded Assets
We explicitly filtered out non-standard or infrastructure-related contracts to prevent data skewing — ENS Registrar (domain names), infrastructure & utility contracts, and Seaport & OpenSea Shared Storefronts (generic aggregate contracts).
Scores are based on current state, not historical peak — so a project that did $15M in volume in 2021 but hasn't traded in 2 years would still be considered dead by today's standards.

03
Methodology

WHAT THE SCORE MEANS

Each collection receives a raw score by summing the points from triggered metrics. However, because dead collections often have missing data, the scoring system uses rescale normalization to produce a final score out of 22 to preserve the score spread.

The process follows three steps:

Step 1 — Calculate Skipped Points
The system identifies metrics that were skipped due to missing data and sums their point values.
Step 2 — Determine Max Available
Skipped points are subtracted from the theoretical maximum to find the max_available points for that specific collection.
Step 3 — Apply the Rescale Formula
The raw score is normalized against what was measurable, then scaled to 22.
Rescale Formula
Rescaled Score = round  Raw Score Max Available  × 22
Example: a collection with 10 points available that triggers 8 of them receives a rescaled score of 18 / 22

Only Dead or Decaying collections are showcased in The Flatline Gallery.

Rescaled Score Verdict
≥ 11 DEAD
7 – 10 Institutional Decay
4 – 6 Stressed / Declining
0 – 3 Alive or Recoverable
Important Exceptions
If the rescaled score is 16 or higher, the collection is marked DEAD even if there is insufficient data (fewer than 8 metrics with data).
Data Insufficient: If the rescaled score is under 16 and fewer than 8 metrics provided data, the verdict is flagged as "Data Insufficient" rather than a specific status. These collections were not included in the gallery because not enough signals were captured to make a reliable judgement.
04
Infrastructure

DATA SOURCES

The scoring system pulls from six independent sources to build a complete picture of each collection's state.

Dune Analytics
Curates the primary dataset of closed-lifecycle collections — trading history, volume, and lifecycle parameters.
OKX NFT Marketplace API
Provides floor prices, bid and listing data, and trade history as the primary market data source.
OpenSea API v2
Serves as a fallback for collections that OKX doesn't index, ensuring broader coverage.
Alchemy NFT API
Handles on-chain ownership data and metadata validation across representative token samples.
Apify Tweet Scraper
Fetches recent Twitter activity to evaluate social presence and engagement patterns.
Discord Public API
Checks whether project invite links resolve to live servers and evaluates member counts.
05
Transparency

LIMITATIONS

No automated system can perfectly capture something as human and nuanced as an NFT project. Here are the key limitations to keep in mind.

Data Availability
Market APIs like OKX and OpenSea index collections they consider worth tracking, which means many abandoned projects return little or no market data at all. We rescale scores to compensate, but a collection scored on four signals is less certain than one scored on ten. This is why we included a confidence layer that tracks exactly where the gaps are. Confidence is assessed across seven dimensions: how many metrics had data (data completeness), whether the signals came from multiple independent categories or just one (signal consistency), how many trades were available to validate market signals (sales depth), how deep the bid data was, what percentage of the token supply was covered by the metadata sample, what fraction of owners we could measure, and how far the score sits from the nearest verdict boundary.
Unreliable Twitter & Discord Information
Twitter accounts that have been suspended, banned, or renamed return no tweet data at all. In those cases, we default to treating the account as silent. This means some collections may be penalised on the Twitter silence metric not because the project went quiet, but because the specific handle in our dataset (obtained from OpenSea) is no longer active. The same applies to Discord invite links — an invalid link may reflect a renamed server rather than a deleted one.
Snapshot Scoring
Every collection in the gallery was scored at a single point in time. Occasionally a collection catches a second wind — a notable sale happens, or a community reorganises under new leadership. A project deemed dead from the analysis could look different six months later.
